# Cross-System Principles

## Overview
Wrap-up of the Simple Poker Systems bundle. The recurring themes across all 12 systems. Internalize these and most decisions follow.

## Key Concepts
- The systems in this bundle are the backbone of private coaching — they target the most common and impactful mistakes
- Win rates improve quickly when the game is broken into small, isolated, manageable components
- Use solvers to stress-test your hypotheses, not to copy answers

## Cross-System Principles
1. **Know your preflop ranges** — every postflop system depends on it. Memorize the perimeter, not every combo.
2. **Bet top, bet bottom, check middle** — the pyramid principle applies to c-betting (S1, S2), barreling (S5), and defending (S9).
3. **Relative > absolute hand strength** — a medium hand can be the nuts if villain is capped (S10, S11).
4. **Block value, unblock bluffs** — whether bluffing (S4) or hero calling (S11), blocker effects differentiate similar-looking hands.
5. **MDF keeps you rational** — it doesn't produce a hand chart in your head; it prevents emotional overfolding (S7, S9).
6. **Board classification first** — high vs low, connected vs disconnected, paired vs unpaired. The bucket determines the system.
7. **Stack depth changes everything** — below 35bb, top pair check-raises (S6). Shallow = bet more, not less (S1).
8. **Use solvers to stress-test, not copy** — formulate your own hypothesis, then check against the solver. Don't copy answers from the back of the book.
9. **Inflection points matter** — villain checking the turn, not raising the flop, small bet sizes — these weaken villain's range and strengthen your relative hand.
10. **Sizing matters** — in position bet bigger (S8), OOP can block-bet small (S10), geometric sizing to get stacks in when villain is capped.
