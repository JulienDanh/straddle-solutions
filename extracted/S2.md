# System 2 — BTN vs BB C-bet & Check

## Overview
BTN opens, BB calls, BB checks. Three buckets replace System 1's two — the BTN's wider range creates a third category. Core skill: classify the flop, c-bet 100% on good boards, build a checking strategy on boards we miss.

## Buckets
- **Ace-high**: c-bet 100% (with risk-factor exceptions)
- **T–K high with a deuce or three**: c-bet 100% (disconnected by the low card)
- **9-high & below** (or boards BTN misses badly): mix ~60/40 — bet top, bet bottom, check middle

## Decision Rules
- Ace-high clean: c-bet 100%. Risk factors: stack depth (deeper → more checking), monotone, paired boards
- T–K high with a deuce or three present: c-bet 100% (board is necessarily disconnected). Risk factors: two low cards (e.g. K63), monotone
- 9-high & below or boards BTN doesn't hit (no 8+ on board): mix — bet strong + weak, check medium. Cannot bet all bluffs — not enough value to carry them
- Boards with no 8+ card: BTN misses badly (offsuit range starts at offsuit 8s). Must check a lot. Bet top, bet some bottom, check middle

## Risk Factors
| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Stack depth (deeper) on ace-high | More checking |
| Monotone | Substantially more checking |
| Paired boards on ace-high | More checking (less concern on T–K high) |
| Second low card (e.g. K63, T53) on T–K high | Slow down — two low cards interact |
| BTN misses board (no 8+ card) | Must check — not enough value to bet range |

## Sizing
- C-bet 100% boards: 25-40% pot (small sizing)
- Checking strategy boards: larger bets appropriate (protection bets with strong hands)
- In position: prefer larger bets when betting (can polarize more effectively than OOP)

## Blocker Effects
- On two-tone boards, consider both suits the villain calls with — not just the obvious flush draw suit
- Blocking the second suit (the one without equity) is a low-risk way to bluff
- High card of the non-equity suit is more valuable than low card (linear ranges)

## Common Leaks
- Checking back boards that should be 100% c-bet (ace-high, T–K high with deuce/three)
- Not recognizing when BTN misses a board entirely (no 8+) — must check, not bet blindly
- Only considering one suit for blocker effects — should consider both calling suits
- Betting too small in position — IP can polarize, should bet larger

## Hand Examples
- **AJJ paired (ace-high, 50bb)**: Paired board = risk factor. Check KQ, QQ, TT-77 (medium). Bet Jx (strong) + trash (64s, 74s). Solver checks ~50%.
- **KJ2 (king-high with deuce)**: C-bet 100%. Solver shows 14% check — simplification to 100% costs ~0% EV.
- **K92 rainbow (king-high with deuce, 23bb)**: C-bet 100%. Shallow stack = more betting. Solver: 100%.
- **K63 (king-high with three, but second card low)**: Risk factor — two low cards. Not 100%. Check medium (QQ, JJ, TT, 9x). Bet strong + weak.
- **543 two-tone (no 8+, BTN misses)**: Must check. Bet top (66+, 87s, A2-A5), check middle (A8, KQ, pairs of 3s/4s), bet some bottom (J8s, T8s with backdoor draws).
- **742 rainbow (no 8+, BTN misses)**: Check. Bet 78s, 88-TT, K7s, sets. Check A8-A10, KQ, pairs. Bluff with T9, J8, Q8 — but can't use all bluffs (not enough value).

## Heuristics
- "Bet top, bet bottom, check middle"
- "If there's no 8+ on the board, BTN probably misses — check more"
- "Consider both suits, not just the flush draw suit"
- "In position can polarize — bet bigger"
- "Miss a 15% check? Costs ~0% EV — just bet range"

## Rules Q&A
- Q: What distinguishes System 2 from System 1?
  Options: [Three buckets instead of two, Ace-high is always a check, No c-betting at all, Only bet monotone boards]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: System 2 adds a third bucket: T–K high with a deuce/three present gets its own 100% c-bet category. BTN's wider range requires this distinction.
- Q: On KJ2, the solver shows 14% check. What should you do?
  Options: [C-bet 100% — the 14% check costs ~0 EV to simplify away, Check 14% of hands, Check all hands, Bet pot-sized]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: Simplifying to 100% c-bet when the solver checks <15% costs approximately 0% EV. Just bet range.
- Q: What makes a board where BTN must check (not c-bet 100%)?
  Options: [No card 8 or higher on the board — BTN's offsuit range misses, The board is paired, The board is monotone, The board has a deuce]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: BTN opens offsuit 8s+. If no card 8+ is on the flop, most of BTN's range is air. Not enough value to bet range — must check.
- Q: On a two-tone board, which suit should you prefer for bluffing?
  Options: [The suit that blocks calls without giving you equity, The flush draw suit, The suit on the board, It doesn't matter]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: Block the second calling suit (the one without your equity). No risk of being check-raised off equity.
- Q: What happens to ace-high c-bet strategy as stacks get deeper?
  Options: [More checking — deeper stacks require more caution, More betting — more money to be made, No change, Switch to pot-sized bets]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: Deeper stacks = more checking on ace-high. Shallow = more betting (overpair asymmetry).
- Q: When BTN checks back a medium-strength hand on a board they miss, what's the risk?
  Options: [The checking range becomes too medium-strength and vulnerable to aggression, There is no risk, The opponent will always fold, The opponent will always raise]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: If BTN only checks medium-strength hands, the range is face-up and OOP can bluff aggressively. This is why even on miss boards, BTN bets some strong hands (traps) and bluffs.
