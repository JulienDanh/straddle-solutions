# System 8 — Bet Sizing In Position

## Overview
Hero is in position and must choose a bet size. Core principle: in position can polarize more effectively (checking doesn't suffer from capped-range problems like OOP does). So IP should bet larger on average. Small bets in position are usually a mistake.

## Buckets
- **Nut ratio high** (villain can have nuts): Bet small — play around their strong hands
- **Nut ratio low** (villain is capped): Bet large — geometric sizing to get stacks in
- **Default IP**: Bet pot or larger, not small. The risk must be worth the reward.

## Decision Rules
- **In position polarizes better**: IP can check the middle without being punished (turn card uncaps range). So IP bets bigger.
- **Nut ratio determines size**: When villain can have the nuts → bet small. When villain is capped → bet large.
- **Geometric sizing**: Bet equal fractions on turn and river to get stacks in (e.g. pot on turn + pot on river = all-in)
- **Small bets in position are usually wrong**: Reopening the action risks being check-raised by hands you beat. The reward (getting called) must justify that risk.
- **Bet big enough that risk = reward**: If you bet 1/3 pot, villain folds 25%. If you bet pot, villain folds 50%. You make 3x when called but they only fold 2x more. Bigger = more profit.

## Risk Factors
| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Villain has high nut ratio (flushes/straights possible) | Bet small — can't overbet into nuts |
| Villain checked back flop/turn (capped) | Bet large — they're weak |
| Deeper stacks | Need bigger bets earlier to get stacks in (compounding) |
| Shallow stacks | Large bets not needed — SPR is already low |
| Monotone boards | High nut ratio → smaller bets |
| Three Broadway cards | High nut ratio → smaller bets |

## Sizing
- Default IP: pot or slightly over pot
- Geometric: pot on turn + pot on river = all-in (for ~45bb stacks with 15bb pot)
- 1/3 pot in IP: almost never correct. Solver doesn't choose it when given the option.
- OOP can bet small (block bets) — IP cannot

## Common Leaks
- Betting too small in position — reopening action with thin value, risking CR by worse hands
- Not accounting for compounding — small flop bets → small turn bets → small river bets = not enough money invested
- Overbetting into high nut ratios — villain has too many strong hands
- Not recognizing when villain is capped (checked back) — should bet large, not small

## Hand Examples
- **QJ♦ on T92♦ → 2♦ turn (EP vs SB, 100bb)**: Medium strength. Should check. Betting 70% is too thin — villain has KJ, flushes, Jx.
- **88 on A72 → 2 turn → 8 river (EP vs BB, 50bb)**: Villain checks turn and river → capped. 88 is near the nuts. Bet 6-8bb, not 2.5bb. Small bet reopens action to CR.
- **JT on KKT → K turn → Q river (EP vs BB, 50bb)**: Don't reopen with Jx — villain can have quads. Check Jx. Bet QQ+ for value to 3/4 pot.
- **K8 on Q72 → Q turn (BTN vs BB, 50bb)**: Villain check-called flop, checked turn. Nut ratio is low. Should overbet pot on turn to set up river shove. Betting 1/3 pot loses 270bb/100 EV.

## Rules Q&A
- Q: Why can in position bet larger than out of position?
  Options: [IP can polarize better — checking doesn't get punished the same way, IP has more information, OOP is always stronger, It's about pot odds only]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: When IP checks, the turn card uncaps their range. When OOP checks, IP can punish immediately. So IP can isolate the middle and check, then bet bigger with polarized range.
- Q: What is geometric bet sizing?
  Options: [Betting equal fractions on each street to get all-in, Betting pot once, Betting 1/3 pot three times, Any large bet]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: Bet the same fraction on turn and river (e.g. pot + pot = all-in). This maximizes EV with strong hands when villain is capped.
- Q: When villain checks back the turn, what does that mean for your river sizing?
  Options: [They're capped — bet larger, They're strong — check, No change, Bet small for protection]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: When villain checks the turn, it's an inflection point — their range is substantially weaker. Your medium hands become strong relative to their range. Bet larger.
- Q: Why is a 1/3 pot bet usually wrong in position?
  Options: [You reopen the action to check-raises from worse hands for little reward, It's too big, It gives too good odds, It's actually correct]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: Small bets risk being check-raised by hands you beat. The reward (thin value call) doesn't justify that risk.
- Q: What determines whether to bet big or small?
  Options: [Villain's nut ratio — can they have really strong hands?, Your hand strength only, The board texture only, Stack depth only]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: When villain can have the nuts (high nut ratio), bet small to play around it. When villain is capped (low nut ratio), bet large to get stacks in.
- Q: How much EV is lost by betting too small with a strong hand when villain is capped?
  Options: [Can be 200-270bb/100 — massive, Very little, About 5bb/100, Nothing]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: Betting 37% pot instead of pot with strong hands when villain is capped cost 160-270bb/100. A pro's entire win rate is ~5bb/100 — catastrophic.
