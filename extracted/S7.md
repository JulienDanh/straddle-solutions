# System 7 — C-bet Folding Flops

## Overview
Hero c-bets the flop, BB check-raises. Which hands defend? Core skill: use MDF calculations to stay rational, identify the fold percentage, then organize the board to find which hands are trash (fold) vs slightly-better-than-trash (defend).

## Buckets
- **MDF calculation**: Bet / (pot + bet) = fold %. Then reduce further because villain's bluffs have equity.
- **Board organization**: Identify the key card (like System 3). Over-cards to the key card defend. Under-cards fold. Paired card is unusable.
- **Identifying trash**: The worst hands fold. Everything else defends. Since fold % is low (15-35%), focus on identifying the small folding region.

## Decision Rules
- Step 1: Calculate MDF: bet / (pot + bet) = fold %. This is the theoretical fold to make napkins indifferent.
- Step 2: Reduce fold % because villain's bluffs have equity. Actual fold is materially less.
- Step 3: Organize the board — find the key card, identify worst hands.
- Step 4: Fold the absolute trash. Defend everything else.
- On ace-high boards: fold more than MDF suggests (villain's range is stronger)
- On low boards: fold less (villain misses too, your range is relatively stronger)
- **Paired boards**: Can't use the paired card (if villain has trips, you're dead). Evaluate the unpaired card.
- **When bet is very small**: Can reach a point where you fold almost nothing (pot odds too good)

## Risk Factors
| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Small check-raise size | Must defend more (better odds, villain risk-reward is favorable) |
| Villain bluffs have high equity | Must defend more than basic MDF (can't make equity indifferent easily) |
| Board where hero has no trips (e.g. JJ3, BTN doesn't open suited 3s) | Villain's bluffs have tons of equity → fold even less |
| Paired board | Evaluate unpaired card only — can't have equity vs trips |
| Large check-raise | Can fold more (worse odds) |

## Sizing
- MDF formula: fold% = bet / (pot after bet)
- Example: CR to 4.2 into 8.4 pot = 33% MDF fold. Actual fold: ~18% (villain has equity).
- When bets are very small (2bb into 6bb), fold % can drop to 2% — almost nothing folds.

## Common Leaks
- Folding hands that are too strong — MDF says defend 65%, ace-high is snap call
- Not doing the MDF calculation — making emotional decisions when facing a big raise
- Overfolding to check-raises — HUD-reading opponents will exploit high fold-to-CR
- Not identifying the worst hands — if you can't identify trash, you can't engineer the fold correctly

## Hand Examples
- **A5 on JJ3 (BTN, 50bb)**: CR to 4bb. MDF fold = 38%. Actual fold = ~18%. A5 is way too strong to fold. Only trash folds (84o, 85o, T7o).
- **K10 on T33 (BTN, 35bb)**: Paired board — key card = T. Anything with two overcards to T is pure call (KQ, KJ, AJ). Fold double-unders (98o, 87o). K10 has an overcard — easy call.
- **109 on K55 (BTN, 25bb)**: Paired board — can't use the 5. Key card = K. Ace + diamond = pure call. 109 is pure fold (disconnected, no equity vs Kx or trips).
- **AJ on 533r (BTN, 35bb)**: CR to 7.3bb. MDF fold = 50%. Actual fold = ~42%. AJ is near the nuts in this context (BTN doesn't have 3s). Snap call. Fold 109, J8 — the worst hands.
- **K2s on 662 (BTN, 25bb)**: CR to 2bb (very small). MDF fold = 25%. Actual fold = ~2%. K2s with BDFD is worth 45bb/100 — pure call. Almost nothing folds.

## Heuristics
- "MDF keeps you rational — it doesn't produce a hand chart in your head"
- "Fold the trash, defend everything else"
- "When in doubt, the bet is too small to fold much"
- "Paired board: evaluate the unpaired card"
- "Villain's bluffs have equity — fold less than basic MDF"
- "If your fold-to-CR is too high, HUD players will crush you"

  Explanation: MDF makes napkins (zero-equity hands) indifferent. But villain's bluffs have real equity. To make equity-containing bluffs indifferent, you must defend more.

  Options: [The unpaired card (T), The paired card (3), Both, Neither]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: If villain has trips (3x), you're dead — no equity. So evaluate the unpaired card. Over-cards to T defend, under-cards fold.

  Correct: 0
  Explanation: At some point, the bet is so small that every hand has enough equity to call. Pot odds dominate.
