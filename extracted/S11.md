# System 11 — Hero Calling

## Overview
Hero faces a river bet with a bluff catcher. Core skill: systematically differentiate between bluff catchers. Not all bluff catchers are equal — some are worth zero (indifferent), some are worth a lot (block value, unblock bluffs). Focus on what to AVOID, not what to call with.

## Buckets
- **Worth-zero bluff catchers**: Block neither value nor bluffs. Indifferent between calling and folding.
- **Valuable bluff catchers**: Block villain's value range AND unblock villain's bluffs. Worth significant EV.
- **Bad bluff catchers**: Block villain's bluffs (making it harder for them to fold) and/or unblock villain's value. Pure folds.

## Decision Rules
- **Three keys**: (1) Range awareness — know what hands you arrive with. (2) Unblock villain's bluffs. (3) Block villain's value (when possible).
- **Focus on what to avoid**: It's easier to identify bad calls than good calls. "How should you NOT bluff?" applies to calling too.
- **Preflop range is critical**: Know villain's RFI perimeter. If BTN opens offsuit 8s and suited 5s, then having an 8 or 9 in your hand blocks their bluffing range (offsuit bluffs). Having a 4 unblocks bluffs.
- **Turn check = inflection point**: When villain checks back the turn, they lose strong hands. Your calling range can widen.
- **Pot odds = bluffing frequency**: If villain bets pot, you need 33% to call. Villain should have 33% bluffs. Your bluff catcher wins vs bluffs, loses vs value. If their range is balanced, the worst callable hand is worth zero.
- **Not all bluff catchers are zero**: The WORST callable hand is worth zero. Better bluff catchers (with blocking effects) are worth a lot — don't fold them.

## Risk Factors
| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Villain's value range is narrow | Easy to block — focus on blocking value |
| Villain's value range is wide | Hard to block — focus on unblocking bluffs |
| Villain's bluffs come from specific suits/regions | Unblock those — avoid cards from those regions |
| Turn check by villain | Inflection point — villain is weaker, call wider |
| Very small river bet by villain | Likely thin value — consider raising |

## Sizing
- Bluffing frequency = pot odds you're getting. Pot bet → 33% bluffs / 67% value.
- If villain's range is imbalanced (too much value), fold. If too many bluffs, call.

## Blocker Effects
- **Block value, unblock bluffs**: The golden rule of hero calling.
- **Preflop range matters most**: BTN opens offsuit 8s + suited 5s. Having a 4 unblocks suited bluff region. Having an 8 blocks offsuit bluff region.
- **High card blocks more than low card** (linear ranges): K7 blocks more value than 72.
- **Example**: On A73 2 K, if villain's value is AK, AQ, AJ and bluffs are 98, T9, J8 → K7 is a great call (blocks KX value, unblocks 8x/9x bluffs). 87 is a bad call (blocks 8x bluffs, unblocks nothing).

## Common Leaks
- Treating all bluff catchers as equal — they're NOT. Some are worth 27% of the pot, others are worth zero.
- Not thinking about villain's preflop range — it determines which cards block bluffs vs value.
- Calling with hands that block villain's bluffs — you make it harder for them to fold.
- Not recognizing when villain's range is imbalanced (too much value, no bluffs) — fold.

## Hand Examples
- **A3♥ on A972K (EP, 4-way, river 1/3 bet)**: Close to a check-raise. Villain's small bet = thin value (KQ, QJ) or A3-A5♥. King-Queen (6 combos) and QJ (12 combos) are twice as likely as A3-A5♥ (3 combos). CR folds A3-A5♥, calls KQ/QJ.
- **A9s on 9642A (4-way, river 7bb into 28bb)**: Fold. No natural bluffs in villain's range. They need 25% bluffs to balance. No suited connectors called the flop multi-way. Range is all value.
- **10-6o on 1062 → J turn (CO checked back) → river 1/4 bet**: Call. Block-bet would be better. Once we check, 10-6 blocks villain's TX value bets. K10, Q10 = thin value. 10-6 blocks those. Lost to K10.
- **72 on A73 2 K (BTN vs BB, 1/2 pot river)**: Call. Villain checked back turn → no AK, AQ, AJ, sets. Value = A4, A5, KQ, QJ. Bluffs = 98, T9, 87, etc. Having a 7 unblocks offsuit 8x/9x bluffs. Having a 2 unblocks suited bluff region. King-7 is great (blocks KX value, unblocks 8x/9x bluffs). 87 is terrible (blocks 8x bluffs).

  Correct: 0
  Explanation: When villain's range is balanced, the worst callable hand is worth zero. But hands that block value and unblock bluffs are worth significant EV. Folding them is a disaster.
- Q: What are the three keys to hero calling?
  Options: [Range awareness + unblock bluffs + block value, Bet size + pot odds + hand strength, Board texture + position + stack depth, MDF + equity + blockers]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: (1) Know what hands you arrive with. (2) Unblock where villain's bluffs come from. (3) Block where villain's value comes from (when possible).
- Q: Why is K7 a better call than 87 on A73 2 K vs a BTN opener?
  Options: [K7 blocks KX value and unblocks 8x/9x bluffs; 87 blocks 8x bluffs, K7 is a higher pair, 87 has a straight draw, They're the same]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: BTN opens offsuit 8s+. Bluffs come from 98, T9, J8 (offsuit 8x/9x region). 87 blocks those bluffs. K7 blocks KX (villain's value) and unblocks 8x/9x (villain's bluffs).
- Q: When villain's value range is wide, what should you focus on?
  Options: [Unblocking bluffs — blocking value is too hard when value is wide, Blocking value, Calling with the best hand, Folding everything]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: When value comes from many regions (thin value bets), it's difficult to block. Focus on unblocking where their bluffs come from instead.
- Q: What does it mean when a villain checks back the turn?
  Options: [Inflection point — they lose strong hands, your calling range widens, They're trapping, They have the nuts, Nothing changes]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: When villain checks the turn on a dynamic board, they no longer have sets, two pair, strong top pair. Your bluff catcher beats more of their range.
- Q: If villain bets pot on the river, what should their bluff frequency be?
  Options: [33% — matching your pot odds, 50%, 25%, 10%]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: Pot bet gives you 33% pot odds. For your bluff catcher to break even, villain bluffs 33%. Value:bluff = 2:1. If villain has more value than 67%, fold.
