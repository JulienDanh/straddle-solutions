# System 9 — Defending Flops

## Overview
Hero is in the BB facing a c-bet. Core skill: distinguish between trash (fold) and hands that look like trash but aren't (defend). Three inputs determine defending width: (1) bet sizing small?, (2) opener's range weak?, (3) hero's range weak? If all three → defend very wide.

## Buckets
- **Easy folds**: Hands dominated by both top and bottom of villain's range
- **Pure calls**: Pairs, gut shots, backdoor flush draws, double-overs, ace-high with BDFD
- **Check-raises**: Hands with direct equity vs villain's value range + backdoor draws + good blockers

## Decision Rules
- **Three inputs for wide defense**: (1) small bet, (2) weak opener range (BTN), (3) weak hero range (BB flat). All three → defend very wide.
- **Compare hands to themselves**: A gut shot with a BDFD is a "super gut shot" — much better than a naked gut shot.
- **Backdoor flush draw > good kicker**: A BDFD is more relevant than a kicker for evaluating bottom pair.
- **High card of the suit > low card**: Queen of diamonds > nine of diamonds (linear ranges — high card blocks more).
- **Rainbow boards require more creative defense**: Fewer BDFDs → must reach for 3-to-straight, over-unders, double-overs more.
- **Two-tone boards clean up the strategy**: Front-door flush draws supplement defense → fewer messy calls needed.

## Risk Factors
| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Small bet (25-33%) | Defend wider — great pot odds |
| Large bet (50%+) | Defend tighter — must fold more pairs and draws |
| BTN opener (weak range) | Defend wider — villain misses often |
| EP opener (strong range) | Defend tighter — villain hits boards |
| Rainbow board | Defend more creatively — fewer obvious BDFDs |
| Two-tone board | Defend cleaner — flush draws supplement |
| Paired board | Can't use paired card — evaluate unpaired card |

## Sizing
- vs 25-33% bet: defend ~70-80% of range
- vs 50% bet: defend ~60-65%
- vs 83%+ bet: defend ~40-50%, fold more pairs and draws

## Blocker Effects
- Ace of spades naked = great check-raise (direct out + flush draw blocker for barreling)
- King of diamonds = great check-raise on two-tone boards (blocks flush draws, has overcard)
- High card of the equity suit > low card (linear ranges)
- On T72 two-tone (diamonds/hearts): Q9 with Q♦ is pure play, Q9 with 9♦ is close, Q9 with no diamond may fold vs bigger bets

## Common Leaks
- Folding bottom pair to small bets — bottom pair with a BDFD is a "super pair," worth hundreds of bb/100
- Not recognizing when all three inputs (small bet + weak opener + weak hero range) align → must defend very wide
- Folding gut shots with BDFDs — these are "super gut shots," much more resilient than naked gut shots
- Not differentiating between similar-looking hands: 97♦ (gut shot + BDFD = play) vs 97o (gut shot, double unders = fold vs big bet)

## Hand Examples
- **97♦ on J105 (hijack, 60bb, half pot)**: "Super gut shot" — gut shot + BDFD. Can turn combo draws on diamond turns. 97o is a pure fold; 97♦ is a call.
- **Q9 with Q♦ on T72 (BTN, 80bb, 30% bet)**: Pure play. Overcard to T, BDFD, blocks villain's diamond calls.
- **J6♠ on T52 (CO, 60bb, 25% bet)**: Pure check-raise. Direct equity vs T (jack), backdoor straight (6), BDFD (spades). Opponent misses this board a lot.
- **K8s on K53 (hijack, 60bb, half pot)**: Mix. Larger bet → fold more pairs. K8s is close to the line.
- **T5o on T52 (BvB, min-raise preflop, half pot)**: Pure call. Blind vs blind ranges are extremely wide. Must defend most pairs.
- **JTo on Q62r (BTN, 80bb, 30% bet)**: Pure call. Three-to-straight + double overs to 6. vs 83% bet → fold without a diamond.

## Heuristics
- "Three inputs: small bet + weak opener + weak hero range = defend wide"
- "Compare hands to themselves — a super gut shot beats a naked gut shot"
- "BDFD > good kicker"
- "High card of the suit > low card"
- "Rainbow = creative defense; two-tone = clean defense"

## Quiz Spots
- Hand: 97♦ on J105, half pot → Call (super gut shot — gut shot + BDFD)
- Hand: Q9 with Q♦ on T72, 30% bet → Pure play (overcard + BDFD + blocks calls)
- Hand: J6♠ on T52, 25% bet → Check-raise (direct equity + backdoor draws + villain misses)
- Hand: JTo on Q62r, 83% bet → Fold (three-to-straight but no BDFD, sizing-sensitive)

## Rules Q&A
- Q: What three inputs produce very wide defending strategies?
  Options: [Small bet + weak opener range + weak hero range, Large bet + strong opener + strong hero, Small bet + strong opener + weak hero, Large bet + weak opener + strong hero]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: When villain bets small, their opening range is wide (BTN), and your range is wide (BB flat), you must defend a very substantial number of hands.
- Q: What makes 97♦ a "super gut shot" on J105?
  Options: [Gut shot + backdoor flush draw — can turn combo draws, It's the nuts, The 9 is an overcard, It blocks trips]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: The BDFD means on diamond turns, you pick up a flush draw + gut shot = combo draw. Much more resilient than a naked gut shot.
- Q: Why is the queen of diamonds more valuable than the nine of diamonds?
  Options: [Linear ranges — high card blocks more combos, It's a higher card, It wins more showdowns, It's not]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: RFI ranges are built top-down (ace high first). The queen blocks more of villain's range than the nine.
- Q: How does a rainbow board change your defending strategy?
  Options: [More creative — fewer BDFDs available, must reach for 3-to-straight and over-unders, Defend tighter, No change, Defend only flush draws]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: On rainbow, BDFDs are only suited combos (scarce). Must supplement with 3-to-straight, double-overs, over-unders. Strategy is messier but wider.
- Q: What happens when the bet size increases from 30% to 83%?
  Options: [Defend tighter — fold more pairs and draws, Defend wider, No change, Only call with sets]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: Larger bet = worse pot odds = defend tighter. QJ without a diamond folds vs 83% but is pure call vs 30%. Sizing-sensitive hands.
- Q: What makes J6♠ a good check-raise on T52 vs a small bet?
  Options: [Direct equity vs T (jack) + backdoor straight (6) + BDFD + villain misses board, It's a pair, It blocks trips, It's the nuts]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: Jack gives direct out vs villain's value (TX). Six gives backdoor straight. Spades give BDFD. Villain (CO) misses this board a lot. All factors align for a CR.
