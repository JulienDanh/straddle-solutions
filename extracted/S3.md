# System 3 — BB vs SB Limp Stab

## Overview
SB limps, BB checks preflop. SB stabs the flop. We defend from the BB. Core skill: identify strategically relevant cards, classify hands as double-overs/over-unders/double-unders relative to the key card, defend 75% of range.

## Buckets
- **Key card identification**: Find the strategically relevant card (not ace, not deuce). Build strategy around over/under relative to that card.
- **Three-to-a-straight**: Always playable — gut shots and open-enders never fold
- **Backdoor flush draws**: Competitive — but not all BDFDs are playable (worst hands still fold)
- **High card defending strategy**: Defend from highest down (ace high → queen high → jack high → etc.)

## Decision Rules
- Identify the key card on the board (the lowest non-ace, non-deuce card)
- Defend all gut shots and three-to-a-straight hands (pure calls)
- Defend all double-overs to the key card
- Fold double-unders to the key card (unless they have gut shots or strong BDFDs)
- Mix on over-unders to the key card — need BDFD or proximity to straight
- Fold ~25% of range (opponent's risk-reward = 1/4 = 25%)

## Risk Factors
| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Paired board | Can't use the paired card (if opponent has trips, you're dead) — evaluate the unpaired card instead |
| Ace on board | Not strategically relevant — everything is under an ace |
| Deuce on board | Not strategically relevant — everything is over a deuce |
| Two-tone board | BDFDs are easier to find → less valuable; need stronger BDFDs to play |
| Rainbow board | BDFDs are scarce → more valuable; defend more BDFDs |
| Lower key card | More double-unders available → can fold more; double-overs less valuable |
| Higher key card | Fewer double-unders → double-overs more valuable; must defend more |

## Sizing
- Opponent stabs ~1bb into ~3bb pot (25% risk-reward). We defend ~75%, fold ~25%.

## Blocker Effects
- High card BDFD is more valuable than low card BDFD (high card blocks more of opponent's range in linear construction)
- On two-tone boards, BDFDs compete with each other — only the best ones play
- On rainbow boards, any BDFD is precious — defend more aggressively

## Common Leaks
- Overfolding — BB win rate is the most important in all of NLHE (92 points of opportunity vs 27 for UTG). Folding too much vs stab = giving money away.
- Defending every BDFD blindly — the worst hands with BDFDs still fold (e.g. 42o with a spade on K72 is a fold)
- Not identifying the worst hand on the board — critical skill. If you can't identify the worst hands, you can't engineer the 25% fold efficiently.
- Underestimating three-to-a-straight value — 76o, 87o, 65o are pure calls on most boards

## Hand Examples
- **K72 two-tone, 70bb**: Key card = K (paired 7 is unusable). High card defending: defend all ace highs, then queen highs, etc. 32o is a fold (no straight, no flush draw, loses to everything).
- **A42 two-tone, 70bb**: Key card = 4 (ace and deuce not relevant). All 3x and 5x are pure calls (gut shots). Worst hands contain a 7 (97, T7). J9 with a heart is pure play — high card + BDFD.
- **K62 two-tone, 20bb**: Key card = 6. Double-unders (54, 53, 43) all have gut shots — pure calls. Fold over-unders (73, 83, 93, T3). 75 with three-to-straight is playable.
- **A72 two-tone, 35bb**: Key card = 7. Double-unders to 7 (63 is worst — fold even with BDFD). 65 playable with BDFD. 86 playable (three-to-straight). Double-overs to 7 (J8, T8, JT) all playable.
- **KQ8 rainbow**: Key card = 8. Double-unders (65, 54, 53, 76, 75, 74) = pure folds. 97 with three-to-straight = pure play. All BDFDs playable on rainbow (scarce).
- **JJ3 two-tone**: Key card = 3 (very low). Anything with a deuce = death sentence (72o even with BDFD folds). T9 with three-to-straight = pure play. 54 without BDFD = pure play.

## Heuristics
- "Ace and deuce are not strategically relevant"
- "Double-overs play, double-unders fold, over-unders need help"
- "Three-to-a-straight never folds blind vs blind"
- "Identify the worst hand on the board — if you can't, you can't defend correctly"
- "Poker is zero-sum — if their stab is profitable, that money comes from you"
- "High card BDFD > low card BDFD"

## Rules Q&A
- Q: Which two cards are NOT strategically relevant for building a defending strategy?
  Options: [Ace and deuce, King and queen, Ten and nine, All cards are relevant]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: Everything has undercards to an ace and overcards to a deuce. They don't help classify hands as overs/unders. Use the middle cards instead.
- Q: On a paired board like K77, which card do you evaluate?
  Options: [The unpaired card (K), The paired card (7), Both equally, Neither]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: If opponent has trips, you're dead — no equity against trips. So evaluate the unpaired card for over/under classification.
- Q: What approximate fold percentage should you target vs a 1bb stab into 3bb pot?
  Options: [~25% (1/4 risk-reward), ~50%, ~10%, ~75%]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: Opponent risks 1 into pot of ~4 total after bet. 1/4 = 25% fold.
- Q: On a rainbow board, how do backdoor flush draws change?
  Options: [More valuable — they're scarce, Less valuable — they're common, No difference, They're all folds]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: On rainbow, BDFDs are harder to find (only suited combos). So they trade at a premium — defend more of them.
- Q: What is the worst hand on A42 two-tone?
  Options: [97 (contains a 7, disconnected), 53 (gut shot), 42 (pair of deuces), 86 (three-to-straight)]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: 3x and 5x have gut shots. 6x has backdoor straights. 7 is the most disconnected card — 97 and T7 are the worst hands. Even with a BDFD, 97 may not play.
- Q: Why is BB win rate more important than UTG win rate?
  Options: [92 points of opportunity vs 27 for UTG, It's easier to play BB, You play more hands from BB, The ante is bigger]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: BB can go from -112bb/100 (folding everything) to -20bb/100 (skilled) = 92 points. UTG: -12 to +15 = 27 points. BB skill matters most.
