# BM4 — Blind vs Blind (Bubble)

## Overview
Blind vs blind on the bubble. Stack relationships drive everything: who covers whom, by how much, and at what stack depth. The same ICM principles from BM2/BM3 apply, but with the added dynamic of positional isolation.

## Buckets
- **BB covers SB (SB covered)**: Very tight when short (9-10bb). Raising has opportunity cost — folding preserves tournament equity. No limping when covered substantially.
- **SB covers BB (BB covered)**: Heavily RFI / open shove. Maximize dollar EV by forcing BB to fold. Don't give BB a free ride at equity.
- **Close stacks (game of chicken)**: Can open wider — the player who gets the first bet in has an advantage.

## Decision Rules
- **BB covers SB, SB short (9-10bb)**: Very tight. Competing opportunity cost — folding may be worth more than raising. Win pots outright; don't let opponents draw out on premium hands.
- **BB covers SB, 15bb+**: Can limp more. Risk-reward for IP isolating worsens as stacks deepen — works in SB's favor.
- **BB covers SB, close stacks**: Open wider — BB is handicapped by ICM risk. Game of chicken.
- **SB covers BB, short**: Open shove aggressively and wider. Leverage chip advantage. Don't limp — maximize fold equity and don't give BB a free ride.
- **SB covers BB, mid stacks (20-30bb)**: Still raise/open shove. More open raising vs ChipEV.
- **SB covers BB, deep (50-85bb)**: More open limping, but still more raising than ChipEV. Less open shoving at 85bb vs 30bb.

## Risk Factors
| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| SB covered substantially | No limping — risk-reward too poor. Must win outright. |
| Short stacks (9-10bb) | Very tight — folding has high $EV. Competing opportunity cost. |
| Close stacks | Game of chicken — first bet has advantage. Can open wider. |
| Deeper stacks (15bb+) | Limping returns for SB. Cold calling may appear for BB. |
| SB covering by heaps | Open shove aggressively — maximize fold equity. |

## Sizing
- SB covering BB short: open shove (win outright, don't give free ride)
- SB covering BB mid: open raise (can shove some, but raising is more common)
- SB covered short: min-raise or fold (no limping — must win outright)

## Heuristics
- "Covering = open shove aggressively, don't give a free ride"
- "Covered = very tight, especially at 9-10bb"
- "Folding has opportunity cost — compare raising vs folding $EV"
- "Close stacks = game of chicken, first bet has advantage"
- "Deeper stacks = limping returns for SB, cold calling for BB"

## Rules Q&A
- Q: When SB covers BB on a short stack, what is the best strategy?
  Options: [Open shove aggressively — maximize fold equity, don't give a free ride, Limp every hand, Fold everything, Min-raise only]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: When covering, leverage your chip advantage. Open shove to win outright and maximize dollar EV. Don't let BB see a flop for free.
- Q: Why is the SB very tight at 9-10bb when covered?
  Options: [Folding has high $EV — competing opportunity cost of continuing in the tournament, SB has bad cards, BB always has aces, Position doesn't matter]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: At 9-10bb covered, folding preserves tournament equity. Raising risks busting near the bubble. The $EV of folding can exceed the $EV of raising.
- Q: What changes at 15bb+ when the SB is covered?
  Options: [Limping returns — risk-reward for IP isolating worsens as stacks deepen, SB shoves more, SB folds more, Nothing changes]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: At 15bb+, the BB's risk-reward for isolating worsens (deeper stacks = more risk to play back). SB can start limping again.
- Q: What happens when stacks are close in BvB (game of chicken)?
  Options: [First bet has advantage — can open wider, Both players fold everything, BB always shoves, SB always limps]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: Close stacks create a game of chicken. Whoever gets the first bet in has a slight advantage because the other risks tournament life to play back.
