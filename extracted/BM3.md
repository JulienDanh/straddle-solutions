# BM3 — Opening Into Covering Stacks

## Overview
You cover the players behind you. When you cover, you can apply ICM pressure on them. Opens widen vs the covered scenario, but you must account for covering stacks elsewhere at the table and who else covers you.

## Buckets
- **You cover BB by heaps**: Open wider — they must risk tournament life to play back. You can also cold call wider from IP when deeper (40bb+).
- **You cover but are covered by others**: Still wider than baseline if you cover the BB — pressure the rest of the table.
- **EP covered by others but covering BB**: Very strong open-folds are not unusual (fold 99, AJs, KQo, A9s) — even when you cover the BB, being covered by others tightens you.

## Decision Rules
- **BTN covering BB by heaps**: Cold call very loose — even vs tight opens, you float and win when opener misses. Blinds are handcuffed (tight opener + your cover).
- **EP, covered by others**: Very strong open-folds are not unusual. Fold nines, AJs, KQo, A9s depending on configuration.
- **Covering BB despite being covered**: Open a bit wider than if BB covers you — you can pressure the blinds.
- **Close stacks (game of chicken)**: If you cover but only by a little, BB can play back — be more cautious.
- **Open shoving**: Less attractive near bubble when covered, especially EP. Shifts stronger (AQ not AJ). Common leak: opening too wide on BTN on short stacks when other shorties are present.

## Risk Factors
| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Covered by other stacks behind | Even if you cover BB, covered by others tightens opens |
| FGS: shorter stacks at other tables | Thinnest hands pass — folding has positive $EV |
| You're the shortest at the table | Loosen — can't rely on others busting |
| Close stacks (covering by little) | Game of chicken — BB can play back |
| Deeper stacks (50-100bb) | Cold calling returns in IP; less open shoving |

## Sizing
- Min-raise dominant — preserve pressure without risking tournament life
- Open shoves less frequent than ChipEV, shift stronger (AQ not AJ)
- 3-bet from covered players is more polar and blocker-heavy

## Heuristics
- "Covering = apply pressure, but mind who covers YOU"
- "Very strong open-folds are not unusual in EP — don't autopilot"
- "Cold calling widens when you cover by heaps and are deep (40bb+)"
- "Folding is not zero EV — shorter stacks elsewhere = thinnest hands pass"
- "Open shoves shift stronger (AQ not AJ) near the bubble"

## Rules Q&A
- Q: When you cover the BB by heaps, what can you do from BTN vs a tight EP opener?
  Options: [Cold call very loose — float and win when opener misses, Fold everything, 3-bet everything, Jam]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: BTN covering BB by heaps can cold call even vs tight opens. The opener can't defend loosely (ICM risk premium). Blinds are handcuffed too.
- Q: What hands might you fold from EP even when you cover the BB?
  Options: [Nines, AJs, KQo, A9s — very strong open-folds are not unusual, Only 22-33, Only offsuit connectors, Nothing — covering means open wide]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: Even when you cover the BB, being covered by others behind tightens your range. In some configs, fold nines, AJs, KQo.
- Q: What happens to open shoving near the bubble when covered?
  Options: [Less frequent and shifts stronger (AQ not AJ), More frequent, No change, Only jam aces]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: Open shoving is less attractive near the bubble, especially in EP. When you do shove, hands shift stronger.
- Q: What is the common leak on the BTN on short stacks?
  Options: [Opening too wide — not mindful of other shorties at the table, Opening too tight, Not shoving enough, Limping too much]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: Be mindful of other shorties. FGS overlay: folding has positive $EV when others may bust before you. Thinnest hands pass.
