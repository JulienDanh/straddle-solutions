# System 4 — River Bluffing

## Overview
Hero reaches the river with air and must decide: bluff or check? Two systems guide the decision. System 1 (prioritize): bluff from the bottom of the range up. System 2 (solver-style): bluff with hands that block villain calls and unblock folds. Prioritize System 1, acknowledge System 2's superiority.

## Buckets
- **System 1 — Bottom-up**: Bluff with the weakest hands first (lowest EV when checking). Simple, reliable, requires range awareness.
- **System 2 — Blocker-based**: Bluff with hands that block villain's VPIP (calls/raises) and unblock their folds. Superior but difficult — must triangulate three variables simultaneously.

## Decision Rules
- **System 1**: Identify the bottom of your range. Bluff with those hands first. Prioritize the lowest card in the hand (not the high card) — 72o before 65o.
- **System 2**: Identify which suits villain calls/folds with. Block calling suits, unblock folding suits. Ensure the hand isn't too high up in the range (opportunity cost of checking).
- **When to use which**: System 1 is the default — use it when ranges are wide and blocker analysis is difficult. System 2 when the board 3-flushes (obvious suit to block) or when ranges are narrow enough to map value/bluffs.
- **Three-Broadway rule**: When 3+ Broadway cards are on board and you opened from EP, there are NO offsuit air hands in your range (linear range doesn't open Q9o, 98o). All bluffs must be suited — scarce supply means bluff more aggressively from the bottom.
- **If you have value bets, you need bluffs**: Any time you identify value, you must also have bluffs (unless ranges are extreme).

## Risk Factors
| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Board 3-flushes | System 2 becomes easy — block the flush suit. One-card bias. |
| Ranges are very wide (BvB) | System 2 is hard — too many ways villain can call. Rely on System 1. |
| Three Broadway cards on board | Offsuit air doesn't exist in EP range — bluffs are scarce, bluff aggressively. |
| Busted straight draw + busted flush draw simultaneously | Very bad bluff — blocks both the straight folds and flush folds. Avoid. |
| Hearts lost on previous streets | Can't wait for ideal suit — hearts were bet on flop/turn. Bluff with what you have. |

## Sizing
- Typical river bluff: 50-67% pot
- Pot-sized bet requires 33% bluffs / 67% value to be balanced
- Overbet when villain's nut ratio is low (they can't have strong hands)

## Blocker Effects
- **One-card bias on 3-flush boards**: Bluff with one card of the flush suit. Blocks flushes AND blocks hero calls (villain calls with one-flush blockers too).
- **Avoid both busted straight + busted flush**: Blocks too many of villain's folding range.
- **High card of blocking suit > low card**: Linear ranges mean high card blocks more combos.
- **Previous street suit bias matters**: If hearts were bet on the flop, you can't have hearts on the river. Track which suits were bet on previous streets.
- **"How should you NOT bluff?"**: Easier to identify what to avoid than what to do. Don't bluff with both suits that villain folds with.

## Common Leaks
- Waiting for the perfect blocker and never bluffing — if you can't have the ideal hand, bluff with what you have
- Not having a bluffing range at all — checking every weak hand means your value bets lose fold equity
- Bluffing with hands too high up — opportunity cost of checking is too high
- Only considering one suit for blocker effects — must consider how villain calls AND folds by suit
- Overestimating ability to use System 2 in wide-range spots — System 1 is the reliable fallback

## Hand Examples
- **86o on AK4 two-tone (BB vs SB, river)**: System 1 — bottom of range, bluff. Avoid clubs (villain calls with clubs) and hearts (villain calls with hearts). 6h is a minor negative but acceptable. Pure bluff.
- **A2s on A23r → river 4c (BTN, 50bb)**: System 1 — QJ is too high up. Too many eligible bluffs, not enough value. QJ is second-nut high card — don't bluff with it. Bluff 96o, T6o, J6d instead.
- **82s on Q106 → turn J → river A (BB vs SB)**: 2x combos are ideal System 2 bluffs — villain folded 2x preflop, so having a deuce unblocks their folding range. Pure bluff 82o.
- **75o with 7♣ on Q106cc → river Tc (BB vs SB)**: One-club bias (System 2). Board 3-flushed — obvious which suit to block. 75o without a club = check. 75o with 7♣ = bluff.
- **J9s on AKQ J T 8 board (EP vs CO)**: Three Broadway cards — no offsuit air in EP range. Bluffs are scarce. System 1 says bluff J9s despite club blocker concerns. "To hell with System 2, I'm bluffing anyway."
- **Q9o on AK4 two-tone (EP)**: System 1 — too few bluffs available with three Broadway. Pure bluff. Lost to K9 of spades (illustrates why unblocking spades matters).

## Heuristics
- "Bluff from the bottom of the range up"
- "Pay attention to the lowest card in your hand, not the highest"
- "If you have value, you need bluffs"
- "When in doubt about blockers, just bluff the worst hands"
- "Three Broadway = no offsuit air = bluff aggressively"
- "How should you NOT bluff? — easier to identify what to avoid"
- "Don't wait for the perfect blocker — it's not realistic"

- Q: Which card in your hand matters most for System 1?
  Options: [The lowest card, The highest card, Both equally, The suit]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: 72o is bluffed before 65o because the deuce has better blocking effects vs linear ranges and lower showdown value. Most people only look at the high card — that's a mistake.
- Q: When the board has three Broadway cards and you opened from EP, what's special about your bluffing range?
  Options: [No offsuit air exists — all bluffs are suited and scarce, More offsuit air than usual, No bluffs available at all, Only ace-high can bluff]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: EP opens linearly. No Q9o, 98o, T9o. All unpaired offsuit hands are Broadway pairs. Bluffs are scarce — bluff more aggressively from the bottom.
- Q: Why is bluffing with both a busted straight draw AND busted flush draw bad?
  Options: [Blocks both the straight folds and flush folds simultaneously, It's actually good, Only the flush draw matters, Only the straight draw matters]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: You block too many of villain's folding range. Better to bluff with just one or neither.
- Q: On a 3-flush board, which system becomes easy to execute?
  Options: [System 2 — obvious suit to block, System 1 — obvious bottom hand, Neither, Both equally]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: When the board 3-flushes, you know exactly which suit to block. One-card bias blocks flushes AND hero calls.
- Q: What happens if you wait for the perfect blocker on the river?
  Options: [You won't have a bluffing range — ideal hands aren't realistic holdings, You'll bluff optimally, You'll bluff too much, Nothing happens]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: If hearts were bet on the flop, you can't have hearts on the river. The "perfect" bluff doesn't exist in your range. Bluff with what you actually have.
