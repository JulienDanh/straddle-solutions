# System 10 — River Value Betting

## Overview
Hero reaches the river with a medium-strength hand. Core skill: distinguish absolute hand strength from relative hand strength. A weak hand in absolute terms (second pair, top pair no kicker) can be the relative nuts if villain's actions have weakened their range.

## Buckets
- **Absolute strength**: How strong your hand is in isolation (sets > two pair > top pair > second pair)
- **Relative strength**: How strong your hand is vs villain's CURRENT range. If villain checked back the turn, your second pair may be the nuts.
- **Inflection points**: Villain's actions (checking turn, small bets, lack of raises) that weaken their range and strengthen your relative hand

## Decision Rules
- **Identify inflection points**: Villain checking back the turn = huge inflection point. They no longer have sets, strong top pairs, two pair. Your medium hand just became strong.
- **When villain checks back turn + checks river**: They're very weak. Bet your medium-strength hands for value — they check back everything you beat.
- **When you can't check-raise**: If your hand is strong but not strong enough to CR, heavily consider betting. Checking lets villain perfectly polarize (bet strong + bluff, check medium).
- **Small bets OOP are viable**: OOP can block-bet small (1/3 pot). IP should not bet small.
- **Block-bet sizing**: 1/3 pot forces villain to defend 75% — your medium hand looks good if they only continue with 25%.
- **Overbet when villain is capped**: If villain can't have strong hands, overbet to get stacks in.

## Risk Factors
| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Villain checked back turn | Huge inflection point — villain is capped, your medium hand is strong |
| Villain didn't raise flop or turn | Less density in really strong hands |
| River card reintroduces strength (fills straights/flushes) | Both players get stronger — be more cautious |
| River card blanks | Villain stays capped — bet more |
| High nut ratio (many draws filled) | Bet small — villain can have nuts |
| Low nut ratio (nothing connects) | Bet large — villain is capped |

## Sizing
- OOP block bet: 1/3 pot (forces 75% defense)
- IP: pot or larger (polarize)
- Overbet when villain is capped and you have relative nuts
- Geometric: equal fractions on remaining streets

## Common Leaks
- Checking back the relative nuts — failing to identify that villain's range is capped
- Confusing absolute with relative strength — "I only have second pair" but villain can't have better
- Betting too small in position — reopening action to CR for little reward
- Not betting because "they'll check back what I beat" — bet yourself to monetize

## Hand Examples
- **Set of Js on 957 → 5 turn (EP vs CO, 100bb)**: Should block-bet small. Villain checks back turn = capped. If you check, villain bets flushes/sets/overpairs (that beat you) and checks medium hands (you beat). You lose value. Block ~30% pot.
- **AQ on J97 → T → 9 river (EP vs SB, 80bb)**: Villain checked back turn (no Jx, sets, two pair). River 9 blanks. AQ is near relative nuts. Bet pot or overbet. Solver: mix between pot and overbet.
- **AK on K72 → 2 turn → 2 river (EP vs BB)**: Villain just called flop and turn (no raises). AK is near nuts — villain has no KK, 77, 22 (would raise). Pure jam.
- **88 on A72 → 2 → 8 river (EP vs BB, 50bb)**: Villain checks turn and river → capped. 88 is near nuts. Bet 6-8bb, not 2.5bb. Small bet risks CR by worse hands.
- **AK on Q72 → T → Q river (EP vs SB)**: Villain checked back turn → underweight Jx. River Q reintroduces some strength but villain's check means they're weak. AQ is a mix, AK bets more, KK/QQ pure bet.

## Heuristics
- "Relative > absolute hand strength"
- "Villain checks back turn = inflection point = your medium hand becomes strong"
- "If you can't check-raise, bet"
- "Block bet OOP = 1/3 pot (forces 75% defense)"
- "When villain is capped, bet large"
- "Checking lets villain perfectly polarize — bet to monetize"

## Rules Q&A
- Q: What's the difference between absolute and relative hand strength?
  Options: [Absolute = how strong in isolation; Relative = how strong vs villain's current range, They're the same, Absolute is always better, Relative is always better]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: Bottom pair can be the relative nuts if villain's range is all ace-high. Your absolute strength (bottom pair) is weak, but your relative strength (100% equity vs their range) is the nuts.
- Q: What is a "strategic inflection point"?
  Options: [When villain's action dramatically weakens their range, making your hand relatively stronger, When you get a new card, When the pot grows, When villain bets big]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: When villain checks back the turn, they no longer have sets, strong top pairs, or two pair. Your second pair just became much stronger relative to their range.
- Q: Why should you bet rather than check a strong medium hand when villain is capped?
  Options: [Checking lets villain perfectly polarize — they bet hands that beat you and check hands you beat, You might get raised, It's safer, Villain will bet worse hands]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: When you check, villain checks back the medium hands you beat and bets the strong hands that beat you + bluffs. You lose value against the hands you beat.
- Q: What size should an OOP block bet be?
  Options: [1/3 pot — forces 75% defense, Pot-sized, Overbet, Check only]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: 1/3 pot means villain must defend 75% of their range. If your hand beats most of their range, this sizing is ideal.
- Q: When should you overbet the river?
  Options: [When villain is capped and you have the relative nuts, When you have a bluff, Always, Never]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: If villain can't have strong hands (checked back turn, no raises), overbet to get stacks in.
- Q: How much EV can checking the relative nuts cost?
  Options: [200-270bb/100 — catastrophic, Very little, About 10bb/100, Nothing — checking is fine]
  Correct: 0
  Explanation: In examples, checking AK when villain is capped cost 270bb/100. A pro's entire win rate is ~5bb/100.
