River, you have air after barreling. Two systems guide river bluffing. What are they?;System 1: bluff from the bottom of the range up (weakest hands first, lowest EV when checking). System 2: blocker-based (block villain calls, unblock folds). Prioritize System 1.
River bluffing. Which card in your hand matters most for System 1?;The lowest card, not the highest. 72o is bluffed before 65o — the deuce has better blocking effects vs linear ranges and lower showdown value. Most people only look at the high card — that's a mistake.
River, EP opener, board has three Broadway cards. What's special about your bluffing range?;No offsuit air exists — EP opens linearly, so no Q9o, 98o, T9o. All unpaired offsuit hands are Broadway pairs. Bluffs are scarce — bluff more aggressively from the bottom, all suited.
River bluffing. You have both a busted straight draw AND a busted flush draw. Should you bluff?;No — blocking both busted straights and flushes blocks too many of villain's folding range. Better to bluff with just one or neither.
River, board 3-flushed. Which system becomes easy to execute?;System 2 — obvious suit to block. One-card bias: bluff with one card of the flush suit. Blocks flushes AND blocks hero calls (villain calls with one-flush blockers too).
River bluffing. You're waiting for the perfect blocker (e.g. a heart) but hearts were bet on the flop. What happens?;You won't have a bluffing range — the ideal hand isn't a realistic holding. If hearts were bet on the flop, you can't have hearts on the river. Bluff with what you actually have.
River bluffing. You have value bets. What does that mean for your range?;You need bluffs. Any time you identify value, you must also have bluffs (unless ranges are extreme). If you have value, you need bluffs.
Turn, you c-bet the flop and got called. You have a medium-strength hand (e.g. pocket nines on K72). What should you do?;Check. Betting ranges should be dense with strong hands and weak hands, void of the middle. Medium hands get called by better and fold worse — no business betting.

In position, why is a 1/3 pot bet usually wrong?;It reopens the action to check-raises from worse hands for little reward. Small bets risk being CR'd off equity. The reward (thin value call) doesn't justify the risk.
In position, villain has high nut ratio (flushes/straights possible). What sizing?;Bet small — ~70% pot. Can't overbet into a range that can have the nuts. Play around their strong hands.
What is geometric bet sizing?;Betting equal fractions on each street to get all-in. E.g. pot on turn + pot on river = all-in. Maximizes EV with strong hands when villain is capped.

River, you have a medium-strength hand. Villain checked back the turn and checks the river. What should you do?;Bet for value — they're very weak. Villain checks back everything you beat. If you check, villain bets hands that beat you and checks hands you beat. You lose value.
What's the difference between absolute and relative hand strength?;Absolute = how strong your hand is in isolation. Relative = how strong vs villain's CURRENT range. Bottom pair can be the relative nuts if villain's range is all ace-high.
What is a "strategic inflection point"?;When villain's action dramatically weakens their range, making your hand relatively stronger. E.g. villain checks back the turn — they lose sets, strong top pair, two pair. Your second pair just became strong.

River, you face a bet with a bluff catcher. Are all bluff catchers worth the same?;No — some are worth 27% of the pot, others are worth zero. Blocking effects matter. The worst callable hand is worth zero, but hands that block value and unblock bluffs are worth significant EV.
River, you face a pot-sized bet with a bluff catcher. What bluff frequency should villain have to be balanced?;33% bluffs / 67% value. Pot bet gives you 33% pot odds. For your bluff catcher to break even, villain bluffs 33%. If villain has more value than 67%, fold.

What does ICM mean for chip equity?;Doubling your stack does not double your equity. Chips do not scale linearly. Doubling from 5,000 to 10,000 chips might take you from $100 to $195, not $200.
What does FGS add beyond ICM?;Future hands — blind cycles, forced all-ins, who busts next. FGS = ICM + N future hands. Captures the cost of posting blinds and the benefit of others busting before you.
What is the hierarchy for applying ICM/FGS models?;ICM baseline → FGS direction → table logic magnitude. ICM gives the baseline. FGS tells you which direction to deviate. Table/stack/position logic tells you how much.
You're 4.5bb on the bubble, posting BB next hand. What happens to your shove range under FGS?;Wider — shove ~25% vs ICM's ~18%. The blind cost (~112bb/100) plus having fold equity now means you should shove wider.
You're 1.5bb on the bubble, forced all-in next hand. What happens to your shove range under FGS?;Tighter — shove ~25% vs ICM's ~49%. No fold equity (you're called regardless). Doubling doesn't help. Fold and let others bust.
You're 15bb HJ on the bubble with a shorter stack at the table. What should you do?;Min-raise only. No open shoves. Open shoving loses ~$18-35 in equity. Preserve tournament life. FGS says the cost of shoving is too high.
Why is open shoving 15bb on the bubble with a shorter stack present a massive leak?;Min-raising is 4-5x better in tournament equity. Open shoving AJo loses ~$17.70. JTs loses ~$35. FGS says preserve tournament life.
On the bubble, BTN open sizing: ICM vs ChipEV?;2.1x (ICM) vs 2.3x (ChipEV). Smaller open in ICM.
On the bubble, SB 3-bet vs BTN: ICM vs ChipEV?;7.35bb (ICM) vs 8.6bb (ChipEV). Smaller 3-bet, yet defense still tighter.
On the bubble, what is the cost of posting BB+ante?;~-112bb/100. Why short stacks should shove before the blind hits.
On the bubble, opens tighten around marginal hands. What type of hands become more valuable as opens?;Ace-blocker hands (Ax, Kx suited). Block 3-bet bluffs. More important than board coverage hands like T9s.
On the bubble, 3-bet value range: ICM vs ChipEV?;Narrows — kings+, AK (queens mix). Much more polar than ChipEV (JJ+, AQ). Drop queens from value range.
On the bubble, 3-bet bluffs: ICM vs ChipEV?;More blocker-heavy. K4s, K5s, A6s, A7s — hands that block opponent's 3-bet attack range (ace blockers). Less board coverage.
On the bubble, what happens to cold calling at shorter stacks?;Fades aggressively. Calling 2bb off 20bb risks 10% of stack preflop. A c-bet puts in 30%+ before the river. Too risky near the bubble.
On the bubble, equal stacks, higher bubble pressure (152 left, 150 paid). What happens?;More IP bluffing. Game of chicken — IP can bluff more. Blocker opens more important. Extremely blocker-heavy bluffing.
On the bubble, you cover the BB by heaps from BTN. What can you do vs a tight EP opener?;Cold call very loose — float and win when opener misses. BTN covering BB by heaps can cold call even vs tight opens. Blinds are handcuffed.
On the bubble, EP covered by others but covering BB. What hands might you fold?;Nines, AJs, KQo, A9s. Very strong open-folds are not unusual. Even when you cover the BB, being covered by others tightens your range.
On the bubble, what is the common leak on BTN at short stacks?;Opening too wide — not mindful of other shorties at the table. FGS overlay: folding has positive $EV when others may bust before you. Thinnest hands pass.
On the bubble, BB covers SB at 9-10bb. Why is SB very tight?;Folding has high $EV. Competing opportunity cost — folding preserves tournament equity. Raising risks busting near the bubble. No limping when covered substantially.
On the bubble, SB covers BB at short stack. What's the best strategy?;Open shove aggressively and wider. Leverage chip advantage. Don't give BB a free ride at equity. Maximize fold equity, force BB to fold.
On the bubble, close stacks BvB. What's the dynamic?;Game of chicken. First bet has advantage. Whoever gets the first bet in has a slight advantage because the other risks tournament life to play back.
On the bubble, SB covered at 15bb+. What returns?;Limping. Risk-reward for IP isolating worsens as stacks deepen — works in SB's favor. At 15bb+, can start limping again.
On the bubble, BB facing an open, covered and short. Why re-steal more, not less?;Think dollar EV, not binary cash/fail. Calling loses ~50%+ postflop. Re-steal shove with Ax wins outright more often. Playing too passive reduces total dollar EV — you cash more but never double.
On the bubble, SB cold-call threshold?;~20bb. Below: ~0% cold calls, raise/fold only. Above: some cold calls begin, especially if BB is short/handcuffed.
On the bubble, BB 3-bet sizing: ICM vs ChipEV?;Size UP. Larger 3-bets deny equity to speculative calls and lower SPR for narrow value. ICM demands denying equity. Much bigger, very polar.
On the bubble, facing a 3-bet. What happens to AQ, JJ, TT that call in ChipEV?;They become folds. ICM amplifies the cost of calling and losing. Most hands that are calls in ChipEV fold on the bubble.
On the bubble, 4-bet/jam range vs a 3-bet?;Very narrow — AA, KK, QQ (mix), AK. Much tighter than ChipEV. The 3-bettor has a strong, polar range.
On the bubble, how to express players-left-to-bubble?;As a percentage of field. 3 out of 30 = 9.1% = moderate. 3 out of 3,000 = 0.1% = extreme. The % determines the magnitude of adjustments.
On the bubble, why do short stack range adjustments matter more?;A mistake on a 9% open costs 10-20% of your stack. A mistake on a 35% open costs a couple %. Short stack precision is more impactful to your bottom line.
On the bubble, what does ICM NOT account for?;Positions of stacks at other tables, who's hitting blinds next, skill edge, future hands. Use FGS logic for directional shifts when you can't run multi-table FGS.
On the bubble, BTN covered by BB. What boards get range bet?;Ace-high Broadway (AKQ, AKJ, AKT, AQT, AJT). These connect well with BTN's range. AQJ has ~20% check but simplifying to range bet costs ~0 EV.
On the bubble, BTN covered by BB. What boards develop a check range?;AJ8 type (middling draws), queen-high, jack-high. Monotone = high check. Fewer boards range bet than ChipEV.
On the bubble, BTN covered by BB. Overbet ~105% pot — when and why?;Multiple gut shot boards (AQ2, Q84, J74). Protection from draws + overcards. Removes hands from villain's check-call range, creating better turn value spots. NOT on open-ended or two-tone.
On the bubble, BTN covered by BB. What happens on low/mid boards?;Little to no leading from BB. Check-shove low boards (strong, polar). Protection > inducing under ICM. Check-call top pair on safe boards (AQx, AKx — jammed strong hands preflop).
On the bubble, BTN covered by BB. What happens to top pair on Q-high or J-high boards?;Check-raise when top pair could be outdrawn. Check-call top pair on ace-high (safe — jammed strong hands preflop, doesn't need protection).
On the bubble, BB covers BTN. What changes about leading?;Much more prevalent on low/pair boards — near pure. Small amount on J64, J75, Q76 type boards. BB's range hits these, BTN's doesn't.
On the bubble, BB covers BTN. What happens to BTN's c-bet frequency?;Lower than ChipEV. Fewer boards range bet. ICM amplifies risk factors. BTN c-bets less, checks more.
On the bubble, covered stack on turns/rivers. What sizing?;Size down. Reduce risk. Non-all-in river sizings — leave tournament equity behind. Stay balanced: same sizing for value and bluffs.
On the bubble, BB covers BTN. What to be mindful of when bluff catching?;Blocker nuances matter more when float ranges are narrow. When opponent doesn't peel wide on the flop, specific suits are less impactful but more critical to get right.
On the bubble, UTG covers BB. C-bet frequency?;>90%. Range bet almost everything. Few checks. Drop a few points when deeper.
On the bubble, UTG covers BB. Why does BB's check-shove on low boards disappear?;Tighter EP range = more risk of running into nuts. 99 on 9-high = check-shove vs LP. Disappears vs EP. BB can't represent as wide.
On the bubble, UTG covers BB. Value threshold for BB flop check-raises?;Very strong. KT on K84 = pure check-call, not a check-raise. Don't get carried away — balance protection with tournament life.
On the bubble, UTG covers BB, 100bb. What changes?;Some low boards check back. BB has better coverage on low boards when deeper. Can't range back with impunity on every board.
On the bubble, UTG covers BB. Non-all-in river sizings — why?;Leave tournament equity behind when called and losing. Play with AI for sizings. Make "asking why" a habit.
On the bubble, UTG covers BB. BB's tighter range on monotone turns — what does it mean?;BB may have MORE flushes on 3-flush turns. After checking flop, donks on monotone turns. BB's tight preflop range = more suited hands = more flushes.
On the bubble, BTN polar opens, short stacks. What drives the overbet on AQ2?;Flush draws, not gut shots. When BTN covers BB, AQ2 rainbow gets large bet. When covered, AQ2 two-tone gets the overbet. The flush draw is the driver.
On the bubble, BTN polar opens. KQ6 rainbow vs KQ6 two-tone — what's the difference?;Vastly different strategies in ICM. In chips, both probably bet. In ICM, two-tone checks much more. Any risk factor in chips gets amplified.
On the bubble, BTN polar opens. Why check back some strong hands (top pair)?;If you only check back weak hands, your range is vulnerable on the turn. Pull some top pair into check-back to protect turn/river range. ICM doesn't like marginal hands in big pots.
On the bubble, BTN polar opens. What happens to monotone flops?;Lots of checking back vs ChipEV. Don't bloat the pot with weak range facing low SPR turn. Better to check than bet small.
On the bubble, BTN polar opens. Jamming flops — when?;More significant than ChipEV. Especially on boards front-loaded with flush draws. Boards like AQ2 two-tone get large bets. Flush draws drive it, not gut shots.
On the bubble, ICM doesn't like putting money in with what type of hands?;Marginal/medium holdings. In chips, bet-calling second pair is fine. In ICM, it's rough. Don't bloat the pot with thin value.
What is the most important win rate in all of NLHE and why?;Big blind. Skilled BB goes from -112bb/100 (walk-away) to -20bb/100 = 92 points of opportunity. UTG only has 27. BB skill is definitively most important.
What is the pyramid principle for betting?;Bet top (nuts), bet bottom (bluffs), check middle (medium strength). Betting ranges should be dense with strong and weak hands, void of the middle. Medium hands get called by better and fold worse.
What does "bet top, bet bottom, check middle" mean for a checking range?;The checking range is primarily medium-strength hands. They have no business betting — called by better, fold worse. Check keeps them as bluff catchers.
What is the difference between absolute and relative hand strength on the river?;Absolute = how strong in isolation (sets > two pair > top pair). Relative = how strong vs villain's current range. Bottom pair can be the relative nuts if villain is capped.
What is the golden rule of hero calling?;Block value, unblock bluffs. Hands that block villain's value range AND unblock villain's bluffs are worth significant EV. The worst callable hand is worth zero, but good blockers are worth a lot.
What does MDF actually accomplish for a player?;Keeps you rational. It prevents emotional overfolding. It doesn't produce a hand chart in your head. The math prevents you from panicking and folding AJ when you should snap-call.
How should you use solvers — copy or stress-test?;Stress-test, not copy. Formulate your own hypothesis, then check against the solver. Don't copy answers from the back of the book. Your strategy should be roughly in agreement with the solver, but reached differently.
What is an inflection point and why does it matter?;When villain's action dramatically weakens their range. E.g. villain checks back the turn — they lose sets, strong top pair, two pair. Your medium hand becomes strong relative to their weakened range.
In position, what's the default sizing principle?;Bet bigger on average. IP polarizes better — can check the middle without being punished (turn uncaps range). Small bets in position are usually a mistake.
What is the relationship between nut ratio and bet sizing?;Nut ratio high (villain can have nuts) = bet small. Nut ratio low (villain is capped) = bet large. Geometric sizing to get stacks in when villain is capped.
On the bubble, what is the relationship between ICM pressure and risk factors?;ICM amplifies ALL risk factors. Any risk factor present in chips gets worse in ICM. Monotone, connectedness, flush draws, paired boards — all increase checking more in ICM.
On the bubble, what is the key difference between chips and ICM for medium-value hands?;In chips, bet-calling second pair is fine. In ICM, it's rough — don't put money in with marginal holdings. ICM doesn't like spots where you stack off with meh value.
